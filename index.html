r
Distance from the centre, in Schwarzschild radii. One radius, rₛ = 2GM/c², is the event horizon: the edge nothing returns from. For a hole with the mass of Sagittarius A*, at the centre of our galaxy, that is about 12.7 million km.
The page opens at 34 radii: about 430 million km out, nearly three times the distance from the Earth to the Sun.
time
How fast a clock held here ticks, compared with one far away: √(1 − rₛ/r). At the horizon it stops.
It opens at 0.985: a clock here falls about 21 minutes behind every day.
escape
The speed needed to get away from here, as a share of the speed of light: √(rₛ/r). At the horizon it is exactly c. That is why nothing escapes.
It opens at 0.171 c: about 51,000 km every second.
Hawking
A black hole is not perfectly black: it glows at T = ħc³ / (8πGMk). With the mass of Sagittarius A*, that is 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ K seen from far away. Hold still closer to the horizon and you measure more, T / √(1 − rₛ/r), without limit. Left alone for about 10⁸⁷ years, the hole would shrink, heat up and end in a flash.
It opens at 1.46 × 10⁻¹⁴ K: some two hundred trillion times colder than the 2.7 K of empty space.
